## 1. Purpose

In SN5 basically all of our ~$5.3k spend went through the lead's personal card, reimbursements were slow, and needs that surfaced late turned into rush-shipping blowouts ($400 of shipping on $120 of ACP rods, PP-08). The team-wide purchasing process actually exists and is fine; composites members just didn't know it. This doc restates it and adds our ordering calendar.

## 2. Scope

All composites purchases and reimbursements; sponsor material requests at outline level.

## 6. Safety

No physical hazards. Financial safety rule: nobody fronts more than they are comfortable waiting ~a month to get back (R1: deposits take up to a month).

## 7. Procedure (names/tiers as of 2025-26; re-verify each season)

### 7.1 Approval

1. **Every purchase gets approval BEFORE buying**, posted in #purchasing: <$50 → subteam lead; $50–250 → division lead; >$250 → President/VP (R1).

### 7.2 Reimbursement

1. Photograph the **itemized receipt**. A card statement alone is not enough.
2. Add the card statement: redact other lines, keep name, address, last-4, the purchase and the date.
3. Amazon orders: the order must show Shipped or Delivered before submitting.
4. Submit the pinned form within **1 month** of purchase. No Venmo, Zelle, cash or gift cards.
5. Categorize under the subteam **using** the material, not the one buying it (R1).

### 7.3 Distributed purchasers

1. The lead designates 2–3 members (with the finance lead's knowledge) as regular purchasers so no single card is the team's float (PP-08).

### 7.4 Order calendar (composites)

1. **Order calendar:** September, bulk consumables + first Easy Composites basket (≥6-week lead, customs margin, PP-02); December, spring-semester basket; rolling, min-stock triggers from CS-011 stock walks. A shared "basket" doc collects wants before each EC order (SN5 practice: "add what you would like by TONIGHT"; make it a standing doc instead).

### 7.5 Rush orders

1. Any order with >2× shipping-to-item ratio needs the lead's explicit sign-off with the alternative (wait/borrow/local) stated. Make the $400-shipping decision consciously.

### 7.6 Sponsor asks

1. **Sponsor asks:** batch material requests through the lead (Airtech/Sigmatex/Coastal/ACP/Rexco contacts live with PM); never let sponsor stock run to zero before asking, since sponsors have lead times too.

### 7.7 Season close

1. All reimbursements submitted by the finance deadline (SN5: July 4 hard cutoff; unpaid-form escalation window to July 14; dates change yearly, check #purchasing).

### 7.8 Standing request: label printer

Filed here rather than left implicit, because CS-001 Rev C's §5 stops being self-funding at exactly one line item.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Item** | Brother PT-D610BT label printer, plus 3 × TZe-251 24 mm laminated tape and 1 × TZe-231 12 mm |
| **Cost** | ~$115 hardware, ~$76 tape. Year one ≈ $191; recurring ≈ $50/yr at a measured ~480 labels per season |
| **Requested by** | Composites |

**Justification.** The shop's current identification method is handwriting on tape, and a standard Sharpie is soluble in the isopropyl alcohol used at every stage of CS-004 and CS-009. So the labels the team already relies on are being erased by the team's own cleaning step. A single mis-identified 8-ply carbon part costs roughly $300–600 in fabric and resin plus about 20 person-hours to remake, and a mis-identified mold costs a machining slot at RFS. Payback is one prevented mix-up.

**Why not cheaper.** The zero-cost path (Avery sheets on the office laser with a packing-tape overlaminate, CS-001 §7.8) is retained as the documented fallback, not eliminated. It works; it is just slower per label and one grade less durable.

**Model choice.** The PT-D610BT over the app-only PT-P710BT because it has an on-device keyboard and runs on AC. In a shop whose roster turns over every May, a printer that only works from one specific person's paired phone stops working the summer that person graduates. Both take identical tape, so it is reversible.
